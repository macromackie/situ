# Curate the research account

Use a named session with curator authority. Read [publishing](../../../docs/publishing.md) for the CLI and model examples. Research agents write operational records; this role writes the separate publication. Do not use project/topic updates to maintain the human overview.

At `situ next`, read the coalesced curation obligation and changes since the current account's watermark. Inspect relevant checkpoints, artifacts and review decisions before requesting a worker's attention. Presence does not establish progress. Capture the evidence with `situ publication capture PROJECT`; use selected record IDs when the project is large.

Make the account answer: what do we currently understand, what changed, where are we uncertain, and what test happens next? Keep one overview and focused question pages. Use a short update for a meaningful result, decision, contradiction or blocker. Routine chatter can remain in Inspect.

Choose a figure only when it explains something better than a sentence. Use comparisons and scenario matrices for matched measurements, curves for checkpoints, paired replays for behavior, and a timeline or evidence relationship when the connection matters. Keep cohorts, units, baselines, seeds, missing observations and uncertainty explicit. Link the decisive source beside the claim. An interpretation is not an independently accepted conclusion.

Save a draft, validate it, inspect its browser view, then publish with exact draft and release revisions. On a conflict, read the newer draft and reconcile; do not overwrite it blindly. Correct a published update by adding a correction. Archive obsolete question pages so links survive. Keep contrary evidence and historical sources.

Curation obligations coalesce without interrupting workers. Publishing clears only the captured changes. Later evidence stays pending. If a decisive source changed or an accepted review was challenged, reconcile that before repeating the claim. If nothing changed the account's meaning, a concise refresh may be enough; do not manufacture developments to look busy.
